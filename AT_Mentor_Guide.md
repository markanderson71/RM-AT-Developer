## How your input shapes the system

### 1. Blind scoring (MA History) — the most important thing you do

You score each session on the official 2026 form **before you see the AI's number**. Six lines, 1–6:

| Section | Line | The question |
|---|---|---|
| MA | **Cause and Effect** | Does he trace body movement → ski performance → outcome, with the *how*, tied to the task? |
| MA | **Evaluate** | Does he compare what happened with what the peer intended, *and* that intent with the ideal? |
| MA | **Prescription** | Coaching cue plus one reason — does it follow from the cause he named? |
| TU | **Desired Performances** | Does he know what the ideal for this task looks like, as blended fundamentals? |
| TU | **Biomechanics / Physics** | Is the physics driving the analysis or just vocabulary? |
| TU | **Equipment** | Does he state the effect of equipment on *this skier's observed* performance (not a recommendation)? |

MA average ≥ 4 and TU average ≥ 4 = Meets Standards.

**What your score does:** it is stored with the AI score you were blind to, becomes an example the scorer is shown on future sessions ("a session Chris scored 2 on Evaluate looked like this"), and feeds the Agreement panel. A score given after seeing the AI's number is stored but never counted — it is anchored.

**Scale:** 1 not observed · 2 beginning to appear · 3 appears, inconsistently · 4 appears regularly at the standard · 5 frequently, beyond required · 6 continuously, superior. A 4 with room to grow is still a 4.

The optional note on the form is read too — a line-specific note ("Evaluate: he never set her intent against the ideal") is the most useful thing you can write.

### 2. Comments (MA History, Journal)

Every comment you write on an MA session is split into single-point statements and stored under your name. When the scorer cites you, Mark sees your words quoted, with the date. Write the point, not the pleasantry.

You no longer "rescore if you disagree" — your blind score *is* the disagreement, and it recalibrates the scorer through the examples it is shown.

### 3. Your Development Assessment (Progress)

Four fields, each mentor's saved separately:

- **What's working** — what he does consistently.
- **Consistent gaps** — the patterns; the examiner probes these first.
- **Progress I've noticed** — what has shifted.
- **Where to challenge or push** *(new)* — the one or two places he stops short, the question you would keep asking, and what "done" looks like before the next exam. The examiner is told to push here.

**Suggest an assessment** drafts the four sections from his sessions, your scores and comments, and his journal — it never reads an AI number on a session you haven't scored. Take what resonates into your fields, in your words; nothing is saved until it is in your fields.

### 4. Statements from your calls (Progress)

Mark records your calls. The AI pulls out the things you said that define AT-level MA — one point each — and they wait here for you. For each one:

- **Cleaned text** on top; **Heard:** underneath is the raw transcript. Words in [brackets] were filled in by the AI, not heard — check them.
- **Flags:** *hedged* (you said "I think" / "maybe"), *check speaker* (the transcript may have mislabelled who said it), *modeled* (a delivery you demonstrated, kept whole).
- **Replaces an earlier statement?** — tick only if this one supersedes something you said before; the old one stops being retrieved.
- **Approve** makes it retrievable and citable. **Edit** rewords it (stays pending). **Delete** removes it for good.

Start with the starred items — they are what the scorer is still missing.

**After each of Mark's exams (5 minutes):** MA History → the session marked *Needs your score* → read → six taps → Submit → glance at the delta → comment on anything the AI missed.

**After each recorded call (10 minutes):** Progress → approve or delete the statements.

**Every few weeks:** Progress → update the assessment, especially *Where to challenge or push*; check Agreement.

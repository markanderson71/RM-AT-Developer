## Assessment scale

1 — Essential elements were not observed or not present.
2 — Essential elements are beginning to appear.
3 — Essential elements appear, but not with consistency.
4 — Essential elements appear regularly at a satisfactory level.
5 — Essential elements appear frequently, above required level.
6 — Essential elements appear continuously, at a superior level.

All sections must average 4 or above to meet the Learning Outcome. Three scored sections, each with its own Section Average: Instructor Decisions & Behavior, Movement Analysis, Technical Understanding. Overall Result: Meets Standards or Does Not Meet Standards.

## Instructor Decisions & Behavior — Professionalism and Self-Management

Learning Outcome: Strengthens the professional environment by adapting to situations and other group members on behalf of themself and their resort. (Continual Assessment)

Needs/Safety: Monitors their own physical and emotional needs, adjusting to the needs of others in the group.

Behavior Management: Adapts to ambiguity, change, and/or challenges that arise in the day.

## Movement Analysis

Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the ability to analyze advanced skiing while addressing the quality and accuracy of desired skiing outcomes to enhance a clinic participants' skiing.

Cause and Effect: Prioritizes cause-and-effect relationships using any combination of skiing fundamentals and ski and body performance details to benefit the skier's understanding relevant to the desired outcome.

Evaluate: Compares the observed performance to the intended specific outcome and includes speed, turn shape, turn size, line, and/or ski-snow interaction as needed.

Prescription: Prescribes specific and accurate peer-level changes to help the skier achieve their specific outcome and affect speed, turn shape, turn size, line, and/or ski-snow interaction as needed.

## Technical Understanding

Learning Outcome: Uses technical expertise to describe how technical, tactical and equipment interactions rooted in physics and biomechanics, are reflective of contemporary skiing to enhance participants' knowledge.

Understanding of Desired Performances: Identifies and describes ideal performance skiing using multiple skiing fundamentals in blended relationships.

Understanding of Biomechanics/Physics: Accurately applies relevant biomechanics and physics principles to explain both the observed skiers' performance and the general mechanics of efficient, effective skiing.

Equipment: Identifies effects of equipment on skier's performance towards desired outcomes and how equipment choice is impacted/interacting with environment.
